Chord menu: three options

The box is on “me” and you just tapped D, so D sits in the box and the decorations row offers D’s other flavours. The sheet takes the keyboard’s place: you switch between the letter keyboard and this “chord keyboard” the way you switch to emoji. Bottom row: the key’s own chords. Middle row(s): chords from outside the key, each in its scale degree’s column. Top row: decorations, which change the chord in the box. A dot marks a chord the song already uses.

A · As sketched

Middle row holds the non-diatonic triads: II, III, iv, VI, ♭VII. Every 7th is a decoration.

One tap: G C D Em Am F A  Two taps: D⁷ G⁷ A⁷ E⁷ B⁷

B · Dominant row

Middle row holds each degree’s 7th, the way bluegrass uses them (II⁷ is three times as common as II; V⁷ twice as common as V).

One tap: G C D D⁷ Em Am G⁷ A⁷ E⁷ F  Two taps: A (A⁷, then plain)

C · Both

Two middle rows: triads, then 7ths above them. Everything common is one tap; the sheet is one row taller, so the song above it is one line shorter.

One tap: all of the above  Cost: 4 rows instead of 3

The same idea in a minor key (option B, Em)

Only 64 curated songs are in minor, but their profile differs: VII (73% of them), III (64%) and a major V (56%) are staples, so V⁷ sits above v and IV above iv.
